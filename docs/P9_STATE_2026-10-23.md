## Measurement gaps that are not quantizer results

Ninety-six 27B rows still store the pre-changelog cap: reasoning 64 (47),
math 256 (40), instruction 256 (9). Every one of those rows stopped before
the cap (`predicted_n` strictly below it). That matches the changelog rule
that a finished short answer is kept. It is not a silent rescore of cut-off
text. The 2B arms do not have this split. All of their rows use the locked
caps. A 27B number and a 2B number are different networks under different
generation budgets, even before the size difference.

Factual `n_predict` is 16. IQ2 hit that cap on 98/100 rows. BF16 hit it on
0/100. The comparison is fair under the locked protocol and is a poor
reading of "knowledge" for the arm that did not finish the letter.

Sample rows store `prompt_ms`, `prompt_per_second`, and
`predicted_per_second`. They do not store a separate TTFT, and they do not
store a suite peak VRAM. Medians of those timing fields are not a speed
protocol. Coding was not re-executed during this audit. The stored pass
bit stands. The checker is still `python -I` under the user account, with
a denylist and a 5 second timeout.

GGUF headers for the four local 2B files share architecture `qwen35`,
320 tensors, 248,320 tokens (same token-list hash), and the same chat
template (SHA-256 prefix `7f0e529032c25183`, 7,816 bytes). Q8, Q4, and IQ2
name `quantize.imatrix.file` as `Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF/imatrix_unsloth.gguf` with
186 entries and 80 chunks. `general.quantized_by` is Unsloth. The header
does not contain revision `f6d5376be1edb4d416d56da11e5397a961aca8ae`; that
revision is only in the local plan note. The headers do not prove that
each quant was built from the BF16 file on disk. They do rule out a
tokenizer or chat-template mismatch between these four files.
